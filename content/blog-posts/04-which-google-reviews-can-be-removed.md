# Which Google Reviews Can Be Removed? Every Policy Violation Explained

**Google removes reviews that break its user-contributed content policy – not reviews that are negative, harsh or unfair. The main categories are fake engagement, conflict of interest, off-topic content, harassment and hate speech, personal information, offensive or sexual content, and illegal content.**

## The policy violations, one by one

| Violation | What it means | Typical example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fake engagement | Not based on a real experience, paid, incentivised or posted by bots | “Never been here, heard it's awful” |
| Conflict of interest | Posted by competitors, current or former employees, or the owner | A rival shop owner's 1-star review |
| Off-topic | Not about the experience at this place | Political rants, complaints about a different business |
| Harassment & hate speech | Attacks on individuals or groups | Insults aimed at a named employee |
| Personal information | Phone numbers, addresses, private details | A staff member's home address |
| Offensive & sexual content | Profanity, obscene or sexually explicit content | Slurs, explicit language |
| Advertising & spam | Links, promotions, repeated content | “Visit my channel for real reviews” |
| Impersonation | Pretending to be someone else | A review posted in a real customer's name |
| Illegal or dangerous content | Content that breaks the law or promotes harm | Threats |

## What doesn't count as a violation

- A genuine 1-star review that's unfair or exaggerated.
- Criticism of prices, waiting times or service.
- A review you disagree with factually – unless it's defamatory, which is a legal matter: see [defamatory reviews](/blog/defamatory-google-review-removal).
- Star-only ratings without text – there's nothing for Google to assess.

## How to use this list

When you report a review, pick the one violation that fits best and be ready to explain why. For fake reviews, follow [our guide to proving they're fake](/blog/remove-fake-google-reviews); for competitors and ex-employees, see [conflict-of-interest reviews](/blog/remove-google-review-competitor-former-employee). If Google rejects the report, the [appeal](/blog/google-review-appeal) is your second chance.

## FAQ

### Can a review be removed just because it's unfair?

No. Unfair isn't a policy violation. It has to break one of the rules above.

### What about reviews in another language?

Language alone isn't a reason. The content still has to break a policy.

### Can old reviews be removed?

Yes. There's no time limit on policy violations, though older reviews usually need more evidence.
