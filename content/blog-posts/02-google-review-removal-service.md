# Google Review Removal Services: What They Can Do, What They Cost, and the Red Flags

**A legitimate Google review removal service can only do what you could do yourself: find reviews that break Google's policies, build the evidence, and report or appeal them properly. What you pay for is experience and time. Anyone promising to remove genuine reviews, or to do it “guaranteed”, is either lying or breaking the rules.**

## What a legitimate service actually does

1. Audits every negative review against Google's policies – see [the full list of violations](/blog/which-google-reviews-can-be-removed).
2. Collects evidence: customer records, timelines, patterns across accounts.
3. Reports each review through the correct channel and tracks the status.
4. Writes the appeal when Google says “no violation” – see [how the one-time appeal works](/blog/google-review-appeal).
5. Escalates defamatory content to a legal removal request when it's warranted.

## What it costs

Pricing models vary a lot. The three common ones:

| Model | Typical price | Risk for you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly retainer | $300–$1,500 / month | You pay whether anything is removed or not |
| Upfront per review | $150–$500 per review | Paid even if Google keeps the review |
| Pay on success | Per removed review | You only pay for results |

## Red flags

- “100% guaranteed removal” – nobody controls Google's decisions.
- Promises to remove honest, genuine reviews.
- Upfront payment with no clear refund terms.
- Offers to “bury” bad reviews with new 5-star ones – that's buying fake reviews, illegal under the FTC rule in the US and the DMCC Act in the UK.
- Cold messages that mention your bad reviews and ask for money – often [review extortion](/blog/google-review-extortion-scam).

## Questions to ask before you hire anyone

1. Which of my reviews do you think qualify, and under which policy?
2. What evidence will you need from me?
3. Do I pay if nothing is removed?
4. Do you ever post reviews or use multiple accounts to flag?
5. Who is the legal entity behind the service?

## Doing it yourself vs. hiring help

For one obvious spam review, report it yourself – [here's how](/blog/how-to-remove-a-google-review). A service is worth it when you have several reviews, a rejected report, or a pattern you can't prove on your own.

## FAQ

### Is it legal to pay for review removal?

Yes, as long as the service only reports reviews that break Google's policies through Google's own channels. Paying to have genuine reviews suppressed or to post fake ones is not.

### Can a service remove a review Google already rejected?

Sometimes – through the appeal with better evidence, or a legal request if the review is defamatory. It can't overrule Google.

### How fast do services work?

Clear cases can be removed within days. Appeals take longer. Anyone quoting a fixed deadline for every case is guessing.
