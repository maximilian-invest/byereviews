# Negative Review Extortion: What to Do When Someone Demands Money to Remove 1-Star Reviews

**If someone posts 1-star reviews and then demands money to remove them – or threatens to post more – that's review extortion. Don't pay and don't negotiate. Screenshot everything, report the reviews and the extortion to Google through its dedicated report path, and report the attempt to the police.**

## How the scam works

1. A wave of 1-star reviews appears, often without text or with generic text.
2. You receive a message on WhatsApp, Telegram or email offering to “fix” it.
3. They demand payment, often in crypto or gift cards.
4. Paying usually leads to more demands, not fewer reviews.

## What to do right now

1. Don't reply or pay.
2. Screenshot the messages, phone numbers, accounts and every review.
3. Report the reviews in your Business Profile.
4. Use Google's report form for review extortion so the case is handled as extortion, not just as single reviews.
5. Report the attempt to your local police or national fraud reporting service.

## If it turns into a wave

Extortion often comes together with a sudden wave of reviews. Read [how to stop review bombing](/blog/google-review-bombing) for the steps that protect your profile while Google investigates, and use [the appeal](/blog/google-review-appeal) for any review that isn't removed on the first try.

## How to tell a scam from a real service

Legitimate services never contact you because of reviews they posted, never demand crypto, and never threaten. See [the red flags of removal services](/blog/google-review-removal-service).

## FAQ

### Will paying make the reviews go away?

Rarely. Extortionists often come back for more or never remove anything.

### Does Google remove extortion reviews?

Reviews posted as part of extortion break Google's policies. Reporting them with the extortion evidence gives Google the full picture.
