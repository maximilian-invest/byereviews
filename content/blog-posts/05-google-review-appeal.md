# Google Review Appeal: “Decision Pending”, “No Violation” and How to Win the One-Time Appeal

**After you report a review, the Reviews Management Tool shows its status. “Decision pending” means Google is still reviewing. “No policy violation” means the report was rejected – and you get one appeal per review. Win it by adding new evidence and naming the exact policy, not by repeating the report.**

## What each status means

| Status | Meaning | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Decision pending | Still under review | Wait – usually a few days |
| No policy violation | Report rejected | File the appeal with evidence |
| Removed | Review taken down | Nothing – check it stays down |
| Appeal rejected | Final policy decision | Only a legal request is left, if it applies |

## How to file the appeal

1. Open the Reviews Management Tool and select the rejected review.
2. Choose to appeal the decision.
3. Name the policy it breaks – see [the list of violations](/blog/which-google-reviews-can-be-removed).
4. Add evidence the first report didn't include: records, screenshots, timeline.
5. Submit and track the status.

## What makes an appeal win

- One clear policy, not five.
- Facts, not feelings: “No customer named X visited on that date” beats “this is unfair”.
- Evidence Google can check – reviewer patterns are especially strong for [fake reviews](/blog/remove-fake-google-reviews).
- Short and specific. Reviewers read a lot of appeals.

## When the appeal is rejected

The policy route is then closed for that review. If the review is defamatory or illegal, a legal removal request is still possible – see [legal removal in the US, UK and EU](/blog/defamatory-google-review-removal). Otherwise, a calm public reply is your best option.

## FAQ

### How long does “Decision pending” last?

Usually a few days, occasionally a couple of weeks. If it's much longer, check that the report was submitted correctly.

### Can I appeal twice?

No. Each review gets one appeal, which is why it should include everything you have.

### Can I report the review again instead?

Reporting the same review again without new information rarely helps. Use the appeal.
