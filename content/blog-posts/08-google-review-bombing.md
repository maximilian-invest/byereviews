# Review Bombing on Google: How to Stop a Sudden Wave of 1-Star Reviews

**Review bombing is a coordinated wave of negative reviews, often triggered by news, social media or a single angry post. Google has protections against unusual review activity and can remove reviews that aren't based on real experiences. Report fast, document the pattern and don't fuel it with public arguments.**

## What counts as review bombing

- Many 1-star reviews in hours or days, far above your normal rate.
- Reviews about a news story or a post, not about a visit.
- Reviewers from other cities or countries.
- Copy-paste wording across accounts.

## The first 24 hours

1. Screenshot the review list with dates – the pattern is your evidence.
2. Report the reviews; off-topic and fake engagement are usually the right violations.
3. Don't reply to each review. One calm statement, if any.
4. Check the Reviews Management Tool daily and note which ones Google removes.

## Why Google usually sides with you

Reviews driven by news or social media rather than a customer experience break Google's rules. Google also watches for unexpected spikes and can limit new reviews for a while. Reviews that remain can often be removed individually as [fake](/blog/remove-fake-google-reviews) or off-topic.

## If someone demands money

A wave followed by a payment demand is extortion – follow [the extortion steps](/blog/google-review-extortion-scam) instead. For any review that survives the first report, use [the appeal](/blog/google-review-appeal).

## FAQ

### Will a review bomb hurt my ranking?

It can dent your average rating and click-through rate for a while. Fast reporting limits the damage.

### Should I turn off reviews?

You can't turn off Google reviews for a Business Profile. Focus on reporting and documenting.
